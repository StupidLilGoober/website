Something I personally believe isn't talked about enough in programming
is what I'd like to call "Programmer's Block".

I'm sure it's not a creative term, and is probably already a very commonly
used one, but that still doesn't change the fact that I don't believe new
programmers are ever really taught how to handle programmer's block. Or more
accurately, they aren't taught properly

Most people, when giving advice to people with programmer's block, tell them
what to make. The problem is, if this isn't something the programmer finds
enjoyable, or if the programmer finishes the project, they're just going to
have programmer's block *again*.

So, what I want to do in this blog, is actually tell you the methods I use
to overcome this problem. I might not be an expert, but as someone who comes
across this problem all the time, I think I have enough experience to form
my own perspective.

## What's Causing It?

The first step to finding the solution to the problem is to find out what the
root of the problem is in the first place. I mean, trying to cure something if
you don't even know what's causing it is pretty hard.

Now, I wish I could give you one, easy and simple answer. The truth is, multiple
things can cause it. Therefore, there are multiple solutions too.

### Stress

I found that trying to write code when you are stressed is **almost impossible**.
So, take a break! It sounds ridiculous, "the solution to writing more code is to
stop writing code?", but it actually works extremely well.

### Perfectionism

A lot of times, I get stuck on the `main()` function of my code, trying
to perfect it. So, just keep this in mind: your first version *should* be imperfect.
Don't try to make it amazing. Your first version should be small, private, and
usually not very good.

### Roadblock

Trying to solve an incredibly hard issue is challenging, and can contribute to
Programmer's Block. As ridiculous as it may seem, explain the issue to someone
as simply as you can. Explaining the issue forces you to organize your thoughts 
and figure out exactly what you do and don't understand.

### Fork in the Road

The next problem is having to decide between multiple options that are equally good,
or have their own upsides and downsides. The best solution to this problem is to just
flip a coin: If the result immediately makes you want to flip the coin again, you've 
probably already made your decision. If not, just stick with the one that the coin
decided on.

### Lack of Interest

Think for a second: **do you actually want to work on this?** If the answer is no,
change the end goal or shelve it entirely. There are hundreds of thousands of projects
you can be working on, and forcing yourself to do something that annoys you isn't smart.

> One exception: probably don't do this if it's your *job*.

### Not Ready Yet

This is going to sound a little mean, but I'll try to say it in a way that isn't.

You might just not be ready for what you are trying to make, and that's okay. My advice
here *isn't* to scrap the project, but to learn more about the subjects you will need
to know for the specific project. This could take a week, a month, or even longer, but
if you truly want to make the project it will be worth it.
