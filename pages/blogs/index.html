Watching Godzilla: Minus Zero With My Family

Recently, I went to the theaters to watch Godzilla: Minus Zero with my family. You may not know this, but my family are pretty big Godzilla fans, and that is the grossest understatement I have ever made. As we speak, my grandma on my step-mom's side is trying to collect all of the Godzilla movies in the mainline series.

Anyway, back on point, this is probably just recency bias but I think that this movie was just as good as Minus One. If you've seen Minus One, you'd know that is extremely high praise.

I also think that it had one of the most brutal fight scenes in the entire franchise, to the point it almost made me uncomfortable (not necessarily a complaint).

Just like the original Minus One, it made me tear up. I don't want to say what happens because some people probably haven't seen it yet, but it's just so good at bringing out emotion. Takashi Yamazaki is turning out to be a creative genius.

I Used to Hate JavaScript, Now I Don't

Hating JavaScript isn't exactly a hot take in the programming scene. It is an extremely hateable language, featuring "genius" design decisions such as NaN, literally meaning "Not a Number", being of the type number. However, I think the biggest problem is that it just isn't being used correctly.

This isn't just a defense piece on behalf of JavaScript, I want to bring something to attention; programmers have this odd obsession with forcing their favorite programming languages into places that they just frankly don't belong.

I used to think that JavaScript was a horrible language, and depending on what you're using it for, it is. But, in my opinion, JavaScript is an extremely good language when it's being used for frontend web development.

Now, I think it could also be a fine language for desktop GUI applications, but if it isn't being used for appearances and UIs, it falls flat.

Now this isn't intended to beat you down for wanting to make something in your favorite language, Minecraft wouldn't exist if Notch didn't use Java, but when it gets to a certain point you do have to admit that there's a problem.

I think there is a clear fix for this. If you want to make a project that your favorite language just isn't suited for, you should learn a new language. I can't force you, but I think it's a decent suggestion.
