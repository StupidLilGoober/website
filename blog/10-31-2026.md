<h2>I Used to Hate JavaScript, Now I Don't</h2>
<p>
  Hating JavaScript isn't exactly a hot take in the programming scene.
  It is an extremely hateable language, featuring "genius" design
  decisions such as <code>NaN</code>, literally meaning "Not a Number", 
  being of the type <code>number</code>. However, I think the biggest problem 
  is that it just isn't being used correctly.
</p>
<p>
  This isn't just a defense piece on behalf of JavaScript, I want to
  bring something to attention; programmers have this odd obsession with 
  forcing their favorite programming languages into places that they just 
  frankly don't belong.
</p>
<p>
  I used to think that JavaScript was a horrible language, and depending
  on what you're using it for, it <strong>is</strong>. But, in my opinion, JavaScript is 
  an extremely good language when it's being used for frontend web development.
</p>
<p>
  Now, I think it could also be a fine language for desktop GUI applications,
  but if it isn't being used for appearances and UIs, it falls flat.
</p>
<p>
  Now this isn't intended to beat you down for wanting to make something in your
  favorite language, Minecraft wouldn't exist if Notch didn't use Java, but
  when it gets to a certain point you do have to admit that there's a problem.
</p>
<p>
  I think there is a clear fix for this. If you want to make a project that
  your favorite language just isn't suited for, you should learn a <strong>new</strong>
  language. I can't force you, but I think it's a decent suggestion.
</p>
